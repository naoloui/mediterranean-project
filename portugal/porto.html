    • Weather

    Porto experiences a Mediterranean climate with mild, rainy winters and warm, dry summers. Average temperatures range from around 8°C (46°F) in January to 25°C (77°F) in August. The city receives most of its rainfall between October and April, while the summer months are typically sunny and dry, making it an ideal time for outdoor activities and exploring the city's attractions.

    Getting around

    • Metro

      The metro system in Porto is an efficient and affordable way to get around the city and its suburbs. It consists of six lines that connect key areas, including the airport, city center, and coastal neighborhoods. The metro operates from early morning until late at night, with frequent service during peak hours. Tickets can be purchased at stations or via a rechargeable Andante card.

      Buses

      Porto has an extensive bus network operated by STCP (Sociedade de Transportes Colectivos do Porto). Buses cover areas not served by the metro and provide connections to suburban and rural locations. Tickets can be bought on board or using the Andante card. Night buses are also available for late-night travel.

      Trams

      Porto's historic tram system offers a charming way to explore the city, particularly the scenic routes along the riverfront and through historic neighborhoods. While the tram network is limited compared to the metro and bus systems, it provides a nostalgic experience for visitors looking to see the city from a different perspective.

      On foot

      Porto's compact city center makes it ideal for exploring on foot. Many of the main attractions, such as the Ribeira district, Dom Luís I Bridge, and Livraria Lello, are within walking distance of each other. Walking allows visitors to fully appreciate the city's architecture, street art, and vibrant atmosphere.

    • Day trips

    • Douro Valley

      The Douro Valley, a UNESCO World Heritage site, is renowned for its stunning landscapes, terraced vineyards, and world-class port wine production. Visitors can take scenic river cruises along the Douro River, visit charming wine estates for tastings, and explore picturesque towns such as Pinhão and Peso da Régua. The valley offers a perfect blend of natural beauty and cultural heritage, making it an ideal day trip from Porto.

      Guimarães

      Known as the "birthplace of Portugal," Guimarães is a historic city located about an hour's drive from Porto. The city boasts a well-preserved medieval center, with highlights including the Guimarães Castle, the Palace of the Dukes of Braganza, and the charming Largo da Oliveira square. Visitors can stroll through narrow cobblestone streets, explore local shops, and enjoy traditional Portuguese cuisine in cozy restaurants.

      Braga

      Braga, often referred to as the "Portuguese Rome," is a city rich in religious history and baroque architecture. Located approximately 55 kilometers north of Porto, Braga is home to the stunning Bom Jesus do Monte sanctuary, which features a monumental staircase and panoramic views of the surrounding area. The city's historic center also offers attractions such as the Braga Cathedral and the Archbishop's Palace, making it a worthwhile day trip for those interested in culture and history.
