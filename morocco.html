Morocco

Colours, spice and desert stars

Marrakech

A sensory overload in the best way possible, Marrakech is a city where ancient traditions meet vibrant modernity. Wander through the bustling souks of the Medina, where the air is thick with the scent of spices and the sound of haggling vendors. Visit the stunning Bahia Palace and the serene Majorelle Garden, once owned by Yves Saint Laurent. As night falls, experience the magic of Jemaa el-Fnaa square, where storytellers, musicians, and food stalls create an unforgettable atmosphere.

Fes & Meknes

Fes, the spiritual and cultural heart of Morocco, is a labyrinthine city that transports visitors back in time. The UNESCO-listed Fes el Bali is a maze of narrow alleys, bustling souks, and historic madrasas. Discover the intricate craftsmanship of traditional artisans, from leather tanneries to pottery workshops. Key sights include the Al Quaraouiyine University, one of the oldest in the world, and the stunning Bou Inania Madrasa. Fes is a city that captivates with its rich history and vibrant culture.

Chefchaouen

Nestled in the Rif Mountains, Chefchaouen is a picturesque town renowned for its striking blue-washed buildings and laid-back atmosphere. Stroll through the narrow, winding streets adorned with vibrant blue hues, and explore the charming squares and local markets. The town offers stunning views of the surrounding mountains, making it a haven for nature lovers and photographers. Don't miss the opportunity to sample traditional Moroccan cuisine at one of the many cozy cafes.

Tangier & Tetouan

Tangier, a city steeped in history and mystique, serves as a gateway between Africa and Europe. Explore the vibrant medina, where narrow alleys lead to bustling markets and historic sites like the Kasbah Museum. Just a short drive away, Tetouan offers a glimpse into Andalusian architecture and culture, with its whitewashed buildings and charming plazas. Both cities boast beautiful beaches, lively cafes, and a rich blend of cultural influences that make for an unforgettable visit. Tetouan is also a UNESCO World Heritage site. It was once the capital of the Spanish protectorate in Morocco, and its medina is renowned for its well-preserved Andalusian architecture and vibrant artisan community.

Essaouira

Essaouira, a charming coastal town, is known for its laid-back vibe, historic medina, and stunning Atlantic beaches. The town's fortified walls and ramparts offer panoramic views of the ocean, while the bustling harbor is filled with colorful fishing boats. Explore the narrow streets of the medina, a UNESCO World Heritage site, where you can find local crafts, art galleries, and seafood restaurants. Essaouira is also famous for its vibrant music scene, particularly the annual Gnaoua World Music Festival. Also known as the Jewish city, Essaouira has a rich Jewish heritage, with several historic synagogues and a Jewish cemetery that reflect the town's diverse cultural history.

Casablanca & Rabat

Casablanca, Morocco's largest city, is a bustling metropolis that blends modernity with tradition. Visit the iconic Hassan II Mosque, one of the largest mosques in the world, perched on the edge of the Atlantic Ocean. Explore the vibrant Corniche area, filled with cafes, restaurants, and nightlife. Just a short drive away, Rabat, the capital city, offers a more relaxed atmosphere with its historic medina, royal palaces, and beautiful gardens. Key sights include the Kasbah of the Udayas, the Hassan Tower, and the Mausoleum of Mohammed V.

Ait Ben Haddou & Atlas Mountains

Ait Ben Haddou, a UNESCO World Heritage site, is a stunning example of traditional Moroccan earthen architecture. This ancient ksar (fortified village) has served as the backdrop for numerous films and TV shows, including "Gladiator" and "Game of Thrones." Explore the narrow alleys and historic buildings that make up this remarkable site. Nearby, the Atlas Mountains offer breathtaking landscapes, traditional Berber villages, and opportunities for hiking and outdoor adventures. The region is perfect for those seeking to experience Morocco's natural beauty and cultural heritage.

Agadir & Souss-Massa

Agadir, a modern beach resort city, is known for its beautiful sandy beaches, vibrant nightlife, and excellent surfing conditions. The city was rebuilt after a devastating earthquake in 1960, resulting in a contemporary layout with wide boulevards and modern amenities. Just south of Agadir, the Souss-Massa National Park is a haven for wildlife enthusiasts, home to diverse bird species and unique flora. Explore the park's scenic landscapes, including coastal dunes, wetlands, and cliffs. Agadir also offers a lively souk where you can shop for local crafts and fresh produce.
